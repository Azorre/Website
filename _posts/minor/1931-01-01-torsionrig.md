Me and a group of 3 others built a test stand for determining the material properties of different shafts under torsion. The torsion rig could test square or round shafts of different diameters and materials. We used an old jewelers lathe as a base, and a moment arm of a known length to apply a predictable torque on one end of the fixed shaft. We used a strain gauge to measure strain, and also measured overall angular deflection.

PICTURE OF LASER POINTER

We used a set of laser pointers at different points on the shaft to measure angular deflection super precisely. As the shaft deflects, the laser pointers are rotated different angles depending on their position on the shaft, and by using some trigonometry, one can determine the angle between them. 

PICTURE OF CLAMPS

The week before the final presentation, the cheap chuck that we were using on either end just broke entirely. We didn't have a big budget to buy good quality chucks, so I designed and made a basic chuck out of a thick steel ring and 4 M6 bolts. It worked surprisingly well.
