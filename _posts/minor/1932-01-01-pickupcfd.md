In my first semester of college, I was placed in a 9 person team and told to create something bio-inspired. Somehow we came up with the idea to create a puffer-fish inspired pickup truck cargo cover that would improve its aerodynamic efficiency. Barely knowing CAD and having never touched a simulation of any kind, I volunteered to learn Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) and run all the simulations for the team.

I found a pickup truck model online and simplified it aerodynamically. We then created a few different designs for a cargo cover for the back of a pickup truck based on the curvature of a puffer fish in different parts of its body. 

I used StarCCM+ to perform steady state simulations at highway speeds, with rotating wheels and moving ground models. I learned a lot about simulation, but I definitely learned more about patience, and the joys of meshing and simulation.

We also fired up our school's dingy old wind tunnel to get some kind of validation of simulation results. The wind tunnel had no instrumentation of any kind, so we used streamers to check for flow attachment, and a janky setup with a weighing scale to measure drag. It was mostly a vibe check since there was no way we could match Reynold's numbers or anything, but somehow it generally agreed with simulation results.
